# Portrait masks and background replacement

In the private browser editor, open **Edit settings**, enable **Replace a person’s background**, and choose the backdrop color. Upload the footage or re-edit the current project. Under **Edit or export timeline**, select an accepted scene and choose **Download scene mask** to export its black-and-white source-aligned matte. White retains the person. The final MP4 includes the composite, other selected edits and the project audio. The source and the optional background model stay on the device during editing.

The worker runs the full-precision MODNet graph locally on a 256 × 256 picture proxy at eight samples per second of retained footage. Native FFmpeg and the browser service use the same graph and foreground thresholds. The model estimates a soft alpha per pixel. The browser retains the original picture when an entire scene lacks sufficiently supported foreground and background, or when a sampled mask removes a detected face center. The native tool makes the foreground and background check. Fine hair, props, motion blur, fast movement between samples, and multiple people can produce imperfect edges. The mask is an estimate. The editor makes no claim about identity.

MCP clients can call **generate_portrait_mask** on project-owned local media. Its validated inputs include a filename, optional source start and end in seconds, and a hex background color. It returns paths to the composite MP4, the source-aligned mask and an observation report. The native output uses 30 fps with the input display dimensions. Browser mask downloads use a 512-pixel long edge at 30 fps. Both use eight independent mask predictions per source second.

## Rendered validation

The ordinary-background fixture pans a public-domain NASA portrait. It contains an AAC audio track. A Linux CPU Chromium run produced a 167-frame H.264 square edit and AAC audio. It accepted 45 mask samples over 5.57 seconds. The recorded inference loop took 15.37 seconds. The full exported mask and picture were independently decoded at 0.4, 2.4 and 5.2 seconds. Mean absolute channel error in near-white mask regions was 1.86–2.44 against the source, on a 0–255 scale. Near-black regions differed by 0.46–0.50 from the requested background color. The sampled facial region retained mean alpha of at least 0.98. These measurements establish compositing behavior on this moving-photo fixture.
